Billah, M. M. M., Sun, S. R.

Machine learning-driven multifactor water quality indexing for aquaculture site suitability assessment: a case study in Khulna Division, Bangladesh

Target journal to be confirmed

Abstract

Rapid expansion of aquaculture in the Khulna Division, Bangladesh, necessitates precise, data-driven site selection to ensure environmental sustainability and economic viability. This study presents a novel, machine learning-driven multifactor indexing framework for aquaculture site suitability assessment. A comprehensive primary field survey was conducted across 100 stratified points to collect a holistic dataset comprising critical physicochemical water and soil parameters — depth, temperature, total dissolved solids (TDS), electrical conductivity (EC), water pH, soil pH, specific gravity, salinity, dissolved oxygen, nitrite, nitrate, total ammonia nitrogen, phosphate, moisture and light — alongside localised socio-economic data. To overcome the spatial limitations of point-based sampling, multiple machine learning algorithms were trained and cross-validated to model the empirical relationships between these ground-truth measurements and high-resolution satellite covariates. The highest-performing model was selected to predict continuous spatial raster surfaces for each parameter across the study area. To eliminate prediction artefacts and standardise the spatial gradient, 400 reference points were randomly extracted from the model outputs and processed using inverse distance weighting (IDW) interpolation. Concurrently, an analytic hierarchy process (AHP) was conducted through structured consultations with government officials, industry practitioners and aquaculture experts to derive the relative importance weights of each factor. These weights were integrated with the IDW-smoothed raster layers via weighted overlay analysis. The resulting aquaculture site suitability index categorises the region into suitability zones, providing a robust, spatially explicit decision-support tool for optimising future aquaculture development.

Billah, M. M. M., Sun, S. R.

Rapid flood inundation mapping in southeastern Bangladesh using Google Earth Engine, Sentinel-1 SAR and machine learning

Target journal to be confirmed

Abstract

In July 2026, relentless monsoon rain and hill torrents triggered severe flash flooding across Chattogram's southern upazilas, leaving over a million people stranded. Satkania, Banshkhali, Chandanaish and Lohagara bore the brunt, with embankment breaches along the Sangu and Dolu rivers submerging homes, roads and health facilities, while the Chattogram–Cox's Bazar Highway and the Keranihat–Bandarban link were cut off, isolating Bandarban Hill District. During such fast-moving disasters, ground access is limited and delayed, making rapid satellite-based flood mapping essential for emergency response. This study maps the flood extent using Sentinel-1 SAR imagery processed on Google Earth Engine. After thermal noise removal, radiometric calibration, terrain correction and speckle filtering, VV and VH backscatter values are used to separate floodwater from land and permanent water bodies. Two classification approaches are compared — pixel-based thresholding and a Random Forest classifier — each validated against independent reference points. The resulting flood maps are overlaid with OpenStreetMap building and road data to estimate structures and transport links affected across the worst-hit upazilas, quantifying infrastructure exposure in one of Bangladesh's most flood-vulnerable hill-drainage regions.

Uzzaman, M. A., Billah, M. M. M., Faysal, F. A.

Integrating satellite-derived data and machine learning for air quality assessment and vulnerability mapping in a rapidly urbanizing South Asian megacity

Elsevier · ISBN 9780443449567 · 2027

Abstract

This study undertakes a comprehensive spatiotemporal analysis of air quality trends in Dhaka, Bangladesh. The focus period of 2019 to 2023 captures pollution dynamics over a recent five-year span of rapid urban change. By integrating machine learning and advanced mapping techniques, a colour gradient was used to visualise fluctuations in pollutant concentrations. Key pollutants including SO₂, NO₂, O₃, PM₂.₅ and CO were monitored for their adverse health impacts. The findings reveal a significant decline in air quality, with pollution hotspots intensifying and shifting spatially over time. In 2019 the western and southwestern thanas — Pallabi, Mirpur and Tejgaon — showed the highest pollutant levels; by 2023 a more uniform but concerning distribution appeared, particularly in central and northern areas such as Turag and Sher-e-Bangla Nagar, while northeastern areas including Gulshan and Khilkhet remained comparatively cleaner. Temporal distributions of PM₂.₅ and gaseous pollutants were analysed alongside their interactions using Pearson correlation, and air quality index trends were examined through scatter plots and time-series analysis. A comparative evaluation of model performance between 2019 and 2023 substantiates these findings and underscores the need for evidence-based policy and sustainable urban planning to arrest air quality degradation in Dhaka.

Uzzaman, M. A., Billah, M. M. M., Faysal, F. A.

Spatiotemporal transformation of meteorological to agricultural drought in northwestern Bangladesh using RS and GIS techniques

Elsevier · ISBN 9780443449567 · 2027

Abstract

Drought is one of the most critical environmental hazards affecting agriculture, water resources and livelihoods in Bangladesh, particularly in its northwestern region. This study performs meteorological and agricultural drought assessments, both individually and jointly, to analyse spatial and temporal variability between 2000 and 2018. Three indices were employed: the Standardized Precipitation Index (SPI) for meteorological drought, and the Vegetation Condition Index (VCI) together with the Standardized Soil Moisture Index (SSMI) for agricultural drought. A Composite Drought Index (CDI) was developed for joint evaluation, while Local Indicators of Spatial Autocorrelation (LISA) identified drought hotspots. Meteorological droughts predominantly occurred during winter months, while agricultural droughts extended across both winter and summer, severely affecting crop-dependent districts such as Dinajpur, Rangpur and Thakurgaon. The CDI highlighted persistent vulnerability in western and northern districts, with frequent hotspots in Naogaon, Rajshahi, Dinajpur and Lalmonirhat. Pearson correlation analysis demonstrated significant linkages among precipitation, soil moisture and vegetation indices, with year-to-year variation in strength. A Random Forest model applied to future scenarios indicates a worsening trend by 2025 and 2035, with severe droughts expanding in intensity and extent — underscoring the need for integrated drought monitoring and adaptive management to protect agricultural resilience and food security.

Uzzaman, M. A., Billah, M. M. M., Faysal, F. A.

Explainable hybrid ensemble modeling with remote sensing and GIS for equity-oriented landslide susceptibility mapping in southeastern Bangladesh

Elsevier · ISBN 9780443449567 · 2027

Abstract

Landslides remain one of the most pervasive geo-environmental hazards in steep and rapidly changing landscapes, where interactions between terrain, lithology, hydrology and anthropogenic influence drive slope instability. This study develops a high-resolution landslide susceptibility map by integrating remote sensing, GIS-based spatial analysis and hybrid modelling. A suite of conditioning factors — slope, curvature, relief, lithology, rainfall extremes, TWI, SPI, NDVI, land use, stream and road proximity and population density — was prepared and standardised for spatial comparability. The analytic hierarchy process (AHP) derived expert-informed weights for an initial susceptibility assessment, and extreme gradient boosting (XGBoost) was implemented to capture nonlinear variable interactions. Integrating expert-driven and data-driven outputs allowed robust evaluation of spatial patterns and susceptibility classes. Results show that XGBoost provides enhanced class discrimination, particularly in areas with complex topographic and hydrometeorological conditions, outperforming purely knowledge-based methods. The final zonation delineates very low to very high risk areas, offering insight for early warning, land-use planning and disaster risk reduction.

Billah, M. M. M., Morsad, G., Zowad, M. A.

Investigating effects of urban form on the urban heat island in mega cities of Bangladesh: a case study of Chattogram

ICCEI-2025 · Paper ID 864

Abstract

This paper explores the impact of urban form on the urban heat island effect in Chittagong City Corporation, Bangladesh, studying the relationship between land surface temperature and several spatial indicators. The influence of six primary components — land use, human activity, surface indices, street and building indicators, and ecological infrastructure — on LST was estimated using Landsat 8 satellite imagery and spatial regression models. Built-up areas, including residential, commercial and industrial land, contributed significantly to increasing LST, while rural land showed no such contribution. Surface indices such as the Normalized Difference Moisture Index and the Normalized Difference Built-up Index showed the largest association with LST, pointing to the crucial role of moisture conditions and built intensity. While built-form density and human activity are associated with rising temperature, natural factors such as vegetation and water bodies exert a larger effect. The study concludes that improving green infrastructure and optimising the spatial arrangement of natural features may reduce urban heat more effectively than managing density or human activity alone.

Zowad, M. A., Morsad, G., Billah, M. M. M.

Assessment of sanitation and water service condition in urban slums of Chattogram: a case study of Moti Jharna slum

ICCEI-2025 · Paper ID 862

Abstract

The Moti Jharna slum in central Chattogram faces severe shortages of basic services: insecure housing, poor infrastructure, and inadequate water and sanitation facilities. This study assesses the quality of sanitation and urban water services and measures resident satisfaction through the Customer Satisfaction Index, field visits, household surveys and focus group discussions. A combined SERVQUAL and analytic hierarchy process methodology was analysed across five dimensions: tangibility, reliability, responsiveness, assurance and empathy. Sanitation and water services meet only 22% and 18% of public expectations respectively, indicating severe dissatisfaction. Reliability emerged as the dimension with the highest dissatisfaction, highlighting critical gaps in fundamental service delivery and the urgent need for practical strategies to improve water and sanitation systems in future urban development planning.

Zowad, M. A., Billah, M. M. M.

Assessing the impacts of sea-level rise on future land use and land cover using SLAMM and ANN: a case study of Chattogram, Bangladesh

ICWFM-2027

Abstract

Bangladesh, a low-lying South Asian country, is highly vulnerable to sea-level rise. Projections estimate that by 2100 SLR in Bangladesh could reach 1.5–2 metres, affecting around 1.5 million people and permanently displacing 13 million. This study examines the potential impacts of SLR on land use and land cover in the Chittagong district, where reduced drainage gradients increase flood and waterlogging risk. The SLAMM model and an artificial neural network were used to predict LULC change for 2050 and 2100. Results indicate significant change across the Chattogram district: water bodies and vegetation decrease, built-up areas increase and barren land declines. The total area affected by SLR rises from 24.72 km² in 2022 to 128.14 km² in 2100, with the largest impact on water bodies (16.5 km² to 63.12 km²) and a substantial increase in affected built-up area (0.61 km² to 10.84 km²). The findings highlight the urgency of sustainable development and effective adaptation measures in the district.

Project overview

The Chakaria Government Shrimp Estate in the Cox's Bazar coastal zone covers roughly 8,080 acres and is one of Bangladesh's strategically important aquaculture areas, farmed extensively since the 1970s. Funded through the World Bank-backed Sustainable Coastal and Marine Fisheries Project, the work transforms the estate into a modern semi-intensive aquaculture hub aligned with Bangladesh's Blue Economy aspirations and 8th Five Year Plan targets.

The topographic survey recorded 37,178 data points, revealing a height range from −2.17 m to 5.41 m (mean 1.71 m). Land use analysis confirmed 63.39% aquaculture, 21.71% water bodies and 7.18% fallow wetlands. The hydrographic survey documented active tidal influence from the Moheshkhali Channel, peaking during the July–August monsoon.

  • Topographic survey processing. Processed 37,178 spot-level points into high-resolution digital elevation models, mapping flood inundation zones and terrain suitability across a dynamic tidal landscape.
  • LULC mapping. Produced detailed land use and land cover analysis covering aquaculture, water bodies, fallow wetland, residential land and road networks.
  • SESIA formulation. Led the strategic environmental and social impact assessment, evaluating bio-ecological zones, soil and water quality, mangrove buffers and World Bank ESF compliance.
  • Transportation analysis. Designed and administered transport surveys, then modelled multi-modal traffic flows and regional road connectivity for the Blue Economy framework.
  • Community engagement. Organised key informant interviews, focus group discussions and participatory rural appraisal sessions with fishermen, DoF officials and local government.
  • Master plan delivery. Synthesised every data stream into the final technical report and 50-year strategic master plan delivered to the Department of Fisheries.

Project overview

UTMIDP covers three geographically and administratively distinct upazila headquarters: Lalmai and Monoharganj in Comilla Division, and Gowainghat in Sylhet Division. Each town brings its own planning challenge — Sylhet's seismically active tea-garden landscape, and Comilla's dense peri-urban growth corridor.

The project produces long-term spatial master plans that integrate GIS analysis, participatory community input, climate resilience zoning and multi-sector infrastructure assessment across engineering, socio-economic and environmental domains.

  • Geodatabase integration. Combined geotechnical profiles, seismological vulnerability layers for the Sylhet high hazard zone, hydrographic survey results and infrastructure data into one centralised GIS geodatabase for multi-criteria analysis.
  • Large-scale field surveys. Directed engineering, socio-economic and transport survey campaigns across three dispersed towns, designing Kobo Toolbox instruments with embedded logic checks and skip patterns.
  • Participatory GIS. Applied PGIS methods across key informant interviews, focus groups and open community mapping to bring local spatial knowledge and risk perception into the zoning framework.

Advisor: Dr Mohammed Sarfaraz Gani Adnan — Leverhulme Fellow, Brunel University London; DPhil, University of Oxford

Derived emissivity-corrected land surface temperature from Landsat 8 TIRS Band 10 imagery across Dhaka and Chattogram, then quantified 20 urban form metrics in six categories — land use, building envelope, street and building indicators, human activity, spectral indices and albedo — using bivariate spatial regression with spatial autocorrelation validation.
