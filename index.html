Billah, M. M. M., Sun, S. R.

Machine learning-driven multifactor water quality indexing for aquaculture site suitability assessment: a case study in Khulna Division, Bangladesh

Target journal to be confirmed

Read abstract

Rapid expansion of aquaculture in the Khulna Division, Bangladesh, necessitates precise, data-driven site selection to ensure environmental sustainability and economic viability. This study presents a novel, machine learning-driven multifactor indexing framework for aquaculture site suitability assessment. A comprehensive primary field survey was conducted across 100 stratified points to collect a holistic dataset comprising critical physicochemical water and soil parameters — depth, temperature, total dissolved solids, electrical conductivity, water pH, soil pH, specific gravity, salinity, dissolved oxygen, nitrite, nitrate, total ammonia nitrogen, phosphate, moisture and light — alongside localised socio-economic data. To overcome the spatial limitations of point-based sampling, multiple machine learning algorithms were trained and cross-validated to model the empirical relationships between these ground-truth measurements and high-resolution satellite covariates. The highest-performing model was selected to predict continuous spatial raster surfaces for each parameter across the study area. To eliminate prediction artefacts and standardise the spatial gradient for multi-criteria evaluation, 400 reference points were randomly extracted from the model outputs and processed using inverse distance weighting interpolation. Concurrently, an analytic hierarchy process was conducted through structured consultations with government officials, industry practitioners and aquaculture experts to calculate the relative importance weights of each evaluated factor. These weights were integrated with the smoothed raster layers via weighted overlay analysis. The resulting aquaculture site suitability index categorises the region into varying suitability zones, providing a robust, spatially explicit decision-support tool for stakeholders optimising future aquaculture development.

Billah, M. M. M., Sun, S. R.

Rapid flood inundation mapping in southeastern Bangladesh using Google Earth Engine, Sentinel-1 SAR and machine learning

Target journal to be confirmed

Read abstract

In July 2026, relentless monsoon rain and hill torrents triggered severe flash flooding across Chattogram's southern upazilas, leaving over a million people stranded. Satkania, Banshkhali, Chandanaish and Lohagara bore the brunt, with embankment breaches along the Sangu and Dolu rivers submerging homes, roads and health facilities, while the Chattogram–Cox's Bazar Highway and the Keranihat–Bandarban link were cut off, isolating Bandarban Hill District. During such fast-moving disasters ground access is limited and delayed, making rapid satellite-based flood mapping essential for emergency response. This study maps the flood extent using Sentinel-1 SAR imagery processed on Google Earth Engine. After thermal noise removal, radiometric calibration, terrain correction and speckle filtering, VV and VH backscatter values are used to separate floodwater from land and permanent water bodies. Two classification approaches are compared — pixel-based thresholding and a Random Forest machine-learning classifier — each validated against independent reference points to assess accuracy. The resulting flood maps are overlaid with OpenStreetMap building and road data to estimate the number of structures and key transport links affected across the worst-hit upazilas. The work contributes a satellite-derived, reproducible flood-extent assessment of this event and quantifies infrastructure exposure in one of Bangladesh's most flood-vulnerable hill-drainage regions.

Uzzaman, M. A., Billah, M. M. M., Faysal, F. A.

Integrating satellite-derived data and machine learning for air quality assessment and vulnerability mapping in a rapidly urbanizing South Asian megacity

Elsevier · ISBN 9780443449567 · 2027

Read abstract

This study undertakes a comprehensive spatiotemporal analysis of air quality trends within Dhaka city, Bangladesh. The focus period of 2019 to 2023 allows examination of pollution dynamics over a recent five-year span, capturing critical changes in the urban environment. By integrating machine learning and advanced mapping techniques, a colour gradient was used to visualise fluctuations in pollutant concentrations. Key pollutants including SO₂, NO₂, O₃, PM₂.₅ and CO were monitored due to their adverse health impacts. The findings reveal a significant decline in air quality, with intensified pollution hotspots shifting spatially over time. In 2019 the western and southwestern thanas — Pallabi, Mirpur and Tejgaon — displayed the highest pollutant levels. By 2023 a more uniform yet concerning distribution of elevated pollution was observed, particularly in central and northern regions such as Turag and Sher-e-Bangla Nagar, while northeastern areas including Gulshan and Khilkhet consistently demonstrated relatively lower levels. Temporal distributions of PM₂.₅ and other gaseous pollutants were analysed alongside their interactions using Pearson correlation, and air quality index trends were examined through scatter plots and time-series analysis. A comparative evaluation of model performance between 2019 and 2023 further substantiated these findings. Overall the study indicates a worsening environmental and public health concern, and underscores the need for evidence-based policy action and sustainable urban planning.

Uzzaman, M. A., Billah, M. M. M., Faysal, F. A.

Spatiotemporal transformation of meteorological to agricultural drought in northwestern Bangladesh using RS and GIS techniques

Elsevier · ISBN 9780443449567 · 2027

Read abstract

Drought is one of the most critical environmental hazards affecting agriculture, water resources and livelihoods in Bangladesh, particularly in its northwestern region. This study performs meteorological and agricultural drought assessments, both individually and conjointly, to analyse spatial and temporal drought variability between 2000 and 2018. Three indices were employed: the Standardized Precipitation Index for meteorological drought, and the Vegetation Condition Index together with the Standardized Soil Moisture Index for agricultural drought. A Composite Drought Index was developed to jointly evaluate conditions, while Local Indicators of Spatial Autocorrelation were applied to identify drought hotspots. Results reveal that meteorological droughts predominantly occurred during the winter months, while agricultural droughts extended across both winter and summer, severely affecting crop-dependent districts such as Dinajpur, Rangpur and Thakurgaon. The Composite Drought Index highlighted persistent vulnerability in the western and northern districts, identifying frequent hotspots in Naogaon, Rajshahi, Dinajpur and Lalmonirhat. Pearson correlation analysis demonstrated significant linkages among precipitation, soil moisture and vegetation indices, although the strength of relationships varied by year. A Random Forest model applied to future scenarios indicates a worsening trend by 2025 and 2035, with severe droughts expanding in intensity and spatial extent. These findings underscore the urgent need for integrated drought monitoring and adaptive management to enhance agricultural resilience and food security.

Uzzaman, M. A., Billah, M. M. M., Faysal, F. A.

Explainable hybrid ensemble modeling with remote sensing and GIS for equity-oriented landslide susceptibility mapping in southeastern Bangladesh

Elsevier · ISBN 9780443449567 · 2027

Read abstract

Landslides remain one of the most pervasive geo-environmental hazards in steep and rapidly changing landscapes, where complex interactions between terrain, lithology, hydrology and anthropogenic influences drive slope instability. This study develops a high-resolution landslide susceptibility map by integrating remote sensing, GIS-based spatial analysis and hybrid modelling approaches. A comprehensive suite of conditioning factors — slope, curvature, relief, lithology, rainfall extremes, topographic wetness index, stream power index, NDVI, land use, stream and road proximity and population density — was prepared and standardised to ensure spatial comparability. The analytic hierarchy process was applied to derive expert-informed weights and generate an initial susceptibility assessment. To strengthen predictive performance and capture nonlinear variable interactions, extreme gradient boosting was implemented as a complementary machine learning approach. The integration of expert-driven and data-driven outputs allowed a robust evaluation of spatial patterns and susceptibility classes. Results demonstrate that the XGBoost model provides enhanced class discrimination, particularly in areas with complex topographic and hydrometeorological conditions, outperforming purely knowledge-based methods. The final susceptibility zonation delineates very low to very high risk areas, offering critical insight for early warning, land-use planning and disaster risk reduction.

Billah, M. M. M., Morsad, G., Zowad, M. A.

Investigating effects of urban form on the urban heat island in mega cities of Bangladesh: a case study of Chattogram

ICCEI-2025 · Paper ID 864

Read abstract

This paper explores the impact of urban form on the urban heat island effect in Chittagong City Corporation, Bangladesh, studying the relationship between land surface temperature and several spatial indicators. The impact of six primary components — land use, human activity, surface indices, street and building indicators, and ecological infrastructure — on land surface temperature was estimated using Landsat 8 satellite imagery and spatial regression models. Results indicated that built-up areas including residential, commercial and industrial land contributed to increasing temperature significantly, while rural land showed no such contribution. Surface indices such as the Normalized Difference Moisture Index and the Normalized Difference Built-up Index showed the largest association with land surface temperature, pointing out the crucial role of moisture condition and built intensity. While built-form density and human activities are associated with increasing temperature, natural factors such as vegetation and water bodies have a larger effect. The study concludes that improving green infrastructure and maximising the spatial arrangement of natural features may be more effective in reducing urban heat than focusing only on the management of urban density or human activity.

Assessment of sanitation and water service condition in urban slums of Chattogram: a case study of Moti Jharna slum

ICCEI-2025 · Paper ID 862

Read abstract

The Moti Jharna slum in the city centre faces severe shortages of basic services: insecure housing, poor infrastructure and inadequate water and sanitation facilities. This study assesses the quality of sanitation and urban water services and measures residents' satisfaction levels through the Customer Satisfaction Index, field visits, household surveys and focus group discussions. A combined SERVQUAL and analytical hierarchy process methodology was analysed across five key dimensions: tangibility, reliability, responsiveness, assurance and empathy. The findings show that sanitation and water services meet only 22% and 18% of public expectations respectively, indicating severe dissatisfaction in the settlement. Reliability was the dimension with the highest dissatisfaction, highlighting critical gaps in fundamental service delivery. These results point to an urgent need for practical strategies to improve water and sanitation systems and to guide future urban development planning.

Zowad, M. A., Billah, M. M. M.

Assessing the impacts of sea-level rise on future land use and land cover using SLAMM and ANN: a case study of Chattogram, Bangladesh

ICWFM-2027

Read abstract

Bangladesh, a low-lying South Asian country, is highly vulnerable to sea-level rise, a key impact of global climate change. Projections estimate that by 2100 sea-level rise in Bangladesh could reach 1.5 to 2 metres, affecting around 1.5 million people and permanently relocating 13 million. This study examines the potential impacts of sea-level rise and land use and land cover change in the Chittagong district, where rising water will reduce drainage gradients and increase flood and waterlogging risk. The SLAMM model and an artificial neural network were used to predict land cover change for 2050 and 2100. The results indicate that the Chattogram district will experience significant change, with a decrease in water bodies and vegetation, an increase in built-up areas and a decrease in barren land. The total area affected by sea-level rise increases from 24.72 km² in 2022 to 128.14 km² in 2100, with the largest impact on water bodies rising from 16.5 km² to 63.12 km², and affected built-up area rising from 0.61 km² to 10.84 km². The findings highlight the urgent need for sustainable development and effective adaptation measures in the district.

Project overview

The Chakaria Government Shrimp Estate in the Cox's Bazar coastal zone covers roughly 8,080 acres and is one of Bangladesh's strategically important aquaculture zones, under traditional extensive shrimp farming since the 1970s. Funded through the World Bank-backed Sustainable Coastal and Marine Fisheries Project, the work transforms the estate into a modern semi-intensive aquaculture hub aligned with Bangladesh's Blue Economy aspirations and 8th Five Year Plan targets.

The topographic survey recorded 37,178 data points, revealing a height range from −2.17 m to 5.41 m with a mean of 1.71 m. Land use analysis confirmed 63.39% aquaculture, 21.71% water bodies and 7.18% fallow wetlands. The hydrographic survey documented active tidal influence from the Moheshkhali Channel, peaking through the July–August monsoon.
